# Generated art — prompts

Everything in `public/art/` is generated by hand, once, and committed. No image API runs in
the product, so nothing here costs anything at runtime.

Two sets: the twelve player characters, and the table surface.

---

# 1. Avatar character set

Twelve chip-creatures, one per seat. These are the app's **only** avatar system: the profile
picks one, and the same image renders at the table, in history and on the share card.

Six are the regulars, drawn from photos they gave. Six are invented to fill the gaps the real
six leave — because six friends who all have dark hair and a beard are not tellable apart at
44px, and the seat ring is exactly where that matters.

## What a chip-creature is

The chip **is** the character: a rounded clay poker chip standing upright, with a face on its
pressed inlay, hair sitting on top of the disc, and the edge spots reading as its rim. Short
stub arms. No legs — the chip sits in a circular frame in the app, so anything below the disc
is cropped away.

**This must not read as a denomination chip.** The app draws real chips everywhere — the
buy-in row, the counter, the chip-set cards — and those are flat, face-on, numbered and never
have a face. The creature is three-dimensional, has hair and eyes, and never carries a number.
Keep those two apart or the seat ring and the buy-in row start looking like the same thing.

## Shared style block

Prepend to every character prompt, unchanged.

```
A single stylised 3D character shaped like an upright poker chip, seen straight on. The
chip's body is a thick rounded disc of soft matte clay with a visible pressed inner
circle, and six evenly spaced rectangular edge spots around its rim in a contrasting
cream. Two small rounded stub arms at its sides.

Its face sits on the pressed inner circle: two simple rounded eyes, a small nose, a calm
closed mouth. Human hair sits on top of the disc as a separate solid shape with clear
silhouette, and facial hair reads as one solid shape, never as individual strands.

Material: soft matte clay-plastic, gentle subsurface warmth, no gloss, no sculpted detail
finer than a fingertip. Lighting: broad soft key from the upper left, deep ambient fill,
and a subtle cool violet rim light along the upper right edge. No harsh speculars, no
neon, no glow.

Composition: centred, facing camera, filling the frame with a small even margin. Fully
transparent background — no circle, no backdrop, no ground, no cast shadow.
```

## Negative prompt

```
numbers, denominations, text, letters, digits, logos, watermark, signature, playing
cards, dice, money, stacks of chips, multiple chips, casino table, green felt, legs,
feet, full body, background, backdrop, circle behind, gradient, vignette, cast shadow,
ground plane, photorealism, human body, human torso, realistic skin, glossy, wet, neon,
glow, lens flare, sparkles, cluttered, busy, extra limbs, deformed, two heads
```

## Output spec

- 1024 × 1024 PNG, **transparent background**. The creature is already a coloured disc, so
  a second coloured disc behind it would fight it — the app frames it in a neutral circle.
- Filenames `avatar-aner.png`, `avatar-nir.png`, … then `avatar-07.png` … `avatar-12.png`.
- **Generate Aner first and pass it back as a style reference for the other eleven.**
  Without that, twelve prompts produce twelve unrelated illustration styles.
- Downscale to 256 × 256 WebP before committing. The current set is 3.2 MB for this job.

never by the face, which disappears below about 60px.

Full prompt for one, as the pattern:

```
<shared style block>

The chip body is coral #F2795B with cream edge spots. On top sits a tall mass of tightly
curled black hair, wide and textured, tapering close at the sides. The face has a thin
black moustache and a small pointed chin beard, and a single small silver stud in one
ear. Warm olive skin tone on the face. Calm, level expression.
```

**Two things settled, so they do not get re-opened:**

- *No pet for Ido.* One accessory each, no exceptions — the rule is what keeps the set
  readable at 44px, and an exception in the brief becomes three exceptions by the twelfth
  prompt.
- *Abstraction level is final.* Hair shape, facial hair and one accessory; nothing finer.
  Nothing finer survives 44px anyway, and it keeps the six regulars as characters rather
  than caricatures of themselves.

## What replaces what

`public/art/chip-01.png` … `chip-12.png` are the previous set. They move to
`public/art/_archive/` in the same commit that points `ChipAvatar` at the new files —
archiving them before the replacements exist would 404 every avatar in the app.

Because the files are named per person now, `src/lib/avatar.ts` changes too: `AVATAR_KEYS`
becomes the twelve new keys, and `avatarKeyForPerson()` keeps hashing a Person id onto one of
them so anyone who never picks still gets a stable face.

# 2. The table surface

The table is currently drawn in CSS — a dark ellipse under a violet radial glow, with an
inset ring for the rail. That is resolution-independent and weighs nothing, and it should stay
as the fallback. This asset replaces it where it counts: real felt grain, a rail with actual
material, and light that falls the way light falls.

## What it has to do

It sits behind up to nine seats positioned on an ellipse, with the pot at the centre. So:
**nothing may compete with the centre or the rim.** No pattern, no logo, no inlay, no
markings. It is a surface, not a picture.

## Prompt

```
Top-down orthographic view of an oval poker table, photographed straight down from
directly above, perfectly centred and symmetrical.

Surface: dark charcoal speed cloth, near-black with a faint cool violet cast, matte and
light-absorbing, with a fine even weave visible only as texture and never as pattern.
Colour around #16151E at the centre, falling to #0D0C13 at the outer edge.

Rail: a padded leather cushion running the full perimeter, deep desaturated charcoal
#26262F, matte, with soft rounded stitching along its inner seam. The rail catches a cool
violet rim light along its upper edge and falls into shadow along its lower edge.

Lighting: one broad soft light hanging above and slightly behind the far edge, so the top
of the rail is brightest, the cloth immediately below it carries a wide soft violet
gradient (#7B5CFF at roughly fifteen percent), and the near edge falls away into shadow.
Gentle ambient occlusion where the cloth meets the rail. No specular hotspots, no
reflections, no visible light source.

Fully transparent outside the oval. No background, no floor, no cast shadow, no
surrounding room.
```

## Negative prompt

```
cards, playing cards, poker chips, chip trays, dealer button, money, dice, text, letters,
numbers, logos, table markings, inlay, betting lines, insurance line, cup holders, chairs,
players, hands, arms, green felt, green baize, red felt, casino carpet, wood grain, floor,
room, background, cast shadow, vignette, perspective, three-quarter view, angled view,
tilted, glossy, reflective, wet, neon, glow, lens flare, sparkles, watermark
```

## Output spec

- **1024 × 1536 PNG, transparent outside the oval.** The oval fills the frame edge to edge
  with no margin.
- Filename `table.png`, downscaled to `public/art/table.webp` at 700 × 1050 before
  committing — the table renders at most 350 CSS px wide, so that is already 2×.
- The component's container is `aspect-[5/8]`; this asset is 2:3. I adjust the container to
  match rather than stretching the weave, since a stretched texture is the one thing that
  will read as fake at this size.

## How it gets used

The CSS gradient stays underneath as the base and the fallback. The image layers on top with
the existing `.felt-panel` glow still applied over it, so the violet light stays a token
rather than being baked into a file — if the accent ever moves, the table follows without
regenerating art.

---

## Why no chip or card art

Both are drawn in code and should stay that way. `PokerChip` renders any body and edge colour
at any size from the maths in `src/lib/chip-art.ts`, including a denomination ink guaranteed to
clear 4.5:1 — a raster can only ever be the colours somebody pre-generated, and this app lets a
host describe their own physical tray. Cards are not rendered anywhere; this is a ledger, not a
game client.

# 3. App icon and wordmark

## The idea

A clock tower, leaning. It carries both meanings of the app's name at once — the landmark the
name nods to, and what "on tilt" means at a poker table — and the lean is what makes it a mark
rather than a picture of a building.

**The clock face is a poker chip.** Same edge spots as `PokerChip` draws, with the hands
reading across it. That is the single detail tying the tower to the product; without it this is
a clock tower for any app.

Two clock hands, positioned to read as roughly **two o'clock** — the hour the settlement
actually happens, and an angle that leans the same way the tower does instead of fighting it.

*Note: this is an original mark. It is not a reproduction of any existing game's building —
only the general idea of a leaning clock tower, which no one owns.*

## Prompt — app icon

```
Flat 2D vector app icon. A simple clock tower seen straight on, leaning about eight
degrees to the right, built from three stacked rectangular blocks that narrow towards the
top, capped by a plain pitched roof — no ornament, no windows, no brickwork, no texture.

Set into the widest block is a large circular clock face rendered as a poker chip: a solid
disc with six evenly spaced rectangular notches cut into its rim, a plain inner ring, and
two straight hands with no numerals, pointing to roughly two o'clock.

Pure flat colour, hard edges, no gradients, no shading, no highlights, no outlines, no
perspective, no depth. Bold enough to read as a solid silhouette at sixteen pixels.
Centred, with generous even margin on all four sides.

Colours: a single warm off-white #F5F6F7 shape on a solid deep charcoal #0B0B0F square.
The clock face's disc is charcoal, its rim notches and hands off-white.
```

## Negative prompt

```
3D, isometric, perspective, depth, shading, gradient, glossy, bevel, drop shadow, inner
shadow, outline, stroke, texture, brickwork, stonework, roof tiles, windows, arches,
numerals, roman numerals, text, letters, words, watermark, signature, realistic,
photograph, render, sky, clouds, ground, landscape, city, buildings, people, playing
cards, multiple towers, ornate, detailed, filigree, gold, neon, glow
```

## Output spec

- **1024 × 1024 PNG.**
- Two crops from the same artwork:
  - `icon.png` — the tower at about 76% of the frame.
  - `icon-maskable.png` — the tower at about **56%** of the frame, so nothing is lost when
    a launcher clips it to a circle or a squircle. Android will cut roughly 20% off every
    edge, and the current icon does not survive that.
- Also export `logo.svg` if the generator can, or I trace the PNG once — the mark is simple
  enough that a vector is worth having for the masthead, where it scales with type.
- Replaces `icon-premium.png`, which is 1.4 MB for an icon.

## The wordmark

The masthead is currently type only. With this mark it becomes a lockup: the tower to the
start of the word, at cap height, leaning into it.

```
TILTED
POKER
```

Set in Rubik, the app's only family — 800 for `TILTED`, and `POKER` in the same size and
weight at `--text-2`, so the second line recedes the way it already does on the home screen.
Tracking at `-0.03em`. No separate display face: a wordmark that needs a font the rest of the
app never loads is a wordmark that never matches the app.

## Where it lands

`src/app/manifest.ts` (both icon entries), the README logo, and the home masthead in
`src/app/page.tsx`.
