# Generated art — prompts

Everything in `public/art/` is generated by hand, once, and committed. No image API runs in
the product, so nothing here costs anything at runtime.

Two sets: the sixteen current player characters, and the table surface.

---

# 1. Avatar character set

Sixteen chip-creatures, one per identity. These are the app's **only** avatar system: the profile
picks one, and the same image renders at the table, in history and on the share card.

Ten are the regulars, drawn from photos they gave. Six are invented to fill the gaps the real
ten leave — because friends who all have dark hair and a beard are not tellable apart at
44px, and the seat ring is exactly where that matters.

## What a chip-creature is

**The chip's pale inlay is the face itself.** Not a face drawn on a disc, and not a head
placed inside a ring — the inlay's own colour *is* the skin, and the features sit directly on
it. There is no separate head shape and no second skin tone anywhere in the image.

That single sentence is the whole design. The first generation missed it and produced a
skin-toned human head inside a coral donut: two circles, one wearing the other. The existing
`chip-10.png` gets it right and is the anchor to match.

The character is a whole little figure — chip body, stub arms, stub legs — the way the old set
was drawn. Hair sits on top of the chip like a wig, overlapping its upper rim.

**It must not read as a denomination chip.** The app draws real chips in the buy-in row, the
counter and the chip-set cards, and those are flat, face-on, numbered and faceless.

## Shared style block

Written for GPT Image 2, which reads short labelled segments better than one long paragraph,
and wants scene → subject → detail → constraints in that order.

```
STYLE: Stylised 3D character render, soft matte clay and painted resin with gentle
subsurface warmth. Rounded forms, no gloss, no sharp edges. Broad soft key light from the
upper left, deep ambient fill, and a subtle cool violet rim light down the upper right
edge. Shallow depth, no reflections.

SUBJECT: A single poker-chip character standing upright and facing the viewer. Its body is
a thick round casino chip seen face on, with a raised outer rim carrying eight evenly
spaced rectangular edge blocks in a contrasting cream, and a large pale inlay disc filling
the centre.

FACE: The pale inlay disc IS the character's face. Its features are applied directly onto
that disc in dark clay: two simple oval eyes with a small highlight, two thick curved
eyebrows, a soft closed mouth. The inlay's own pale colour serves as the skin — there is
no separate head shape, no head outline, and no second skin tone in the image.

LIMBS: Two short rounded stub arms at the sides and two short rounded stub legs below, all
in the chip's body colour, like a small toy figure.

COMPOSITION: Centred, filling the frame with a small even margin, shot straight on at eye
level. Transparent background.

CONSTRAINTS: No numbers, no denominations, no text, no letters, no logos, no watermark.
No separate human head, no realistic skin, no neck, no shoulders, no torso. No playing
cards, no dice, no table, no other chips. No background, no ground, no cast shadow.
```

## Anchor first

GPT Image 2 keeps a series consistent by locking a **character anchor** before the rest. So:

1. Generate **Aner** alone until he is right.
2. For each of the other fifteen, attach Aner as a reference image and lead with
   *"Same character style, same chip construction, same lighting as the reference. Change
   only the hair, the facial hair, the body colour and the accessory. Keep everything else
   identical."*
3. Re-state the FACE segment every time — it is the part that drifts back into a human head.

Small single changes per image, never a rewritten prompt between them.

## Output spec

- Generate square source art, then remove the flat chroma background to genuine transparency.
  The creature is already a coloured disc, so a second coloured disc behind it would fight it.
- Filenames use identity keys: `avatar-aner.webp`, `avatar-nir.webp`, …,
  `avatar-yaniv.webp`, plus the six open-character names.
- **Generate Aner first and pass it back as a style reference for the other fifteen.**
  Without that, sixteen prompts produce sixteen unrelated illustration styles.
- Downscale to 256 × 256 WebP before committing.
- Downscale to 256 × 256 WebP before committing. The current set is 3.2 MB for this job.

**Two things settled, so they do not get re-opened:**

- *No pet for Ido.* One accessory each, no exceptions — the rule is what keeps the set
  readable at 44px, and an exception in the brief becomes three exceptions by the twelfth
  prompt.
- *Abstraction level is final.* Hair shape, facial hair and one accessory; nothing finer.
  Nothing finer survives 44px anyway, and it keeps the ten regulars as characters rather
  than caricatures of themselves.

## What replaces what

`public/art/chip-01.png` … `chip-12.png` are the previous set. They move to
`public/art/_archive/` in the same commit that points `ChipAvatar` at the new files —
archiving them before the replacements exist would 404 every avatar in the app.

Because the files are named per person now, `src/lib/avatar.ts` carries the ten reserved keys,
the six open keys and the archived legacy extras. `avatarKeyForPerson()` hashes only across
selectable open characters so anyone who never picks still gets a stable, unreserved face.

# 2. The table surface

The table is currently drawn in CSS — a dark ellipse under a violet radial glow, with an
inset ring for the rail. That is resolution-independent and weighs nothing, and it should stay
as the fallback. This asset replaces it where it counts: real felt grain, a rail with actual
material, and light that falls the way light falls.

## What it has to do

It sits behind up to nine seats positioned on an ellipse, with the pot at the centre. So:
**nothing may compete with the centre or the rim.** No pattern, no logo, no inlay, no
markings. It is a surface, not a picture.

## Prompt

```
Top-down orthographic view of an oval poker table, photographed straight down from
directly above, perfectly centred and symmetrical.

Surface: dark charcoal speed cloth, near-black with a faint cool violet cast, matte and
light-absorbing, with a fine even weave visible only as texture and never as pattern.
Colour around #16151E at the centre, falling to #0D0C13 at the outer edge.

Rail: a padded leather cushion running the full perimeter, deep desaturated charcoal
#26262F, matte, with soft rounded stitching along its inner seam. The rail catches a cool
violet rim light along its upper edge and falls into shadow along its lower edge.

Lighting: one broad soft light hanging above and slightly behind the far edge, so the top
of the rail is brightest, the cloth immediately below it carries a wide soft violet
gradient (#7B5CFF at roughly fifteen percent), and the near edge falls away into shadow.
Gentle ambient occlusion where the cloth meets the rail. No specular hotspots, no
reflections, no visible light source.

Fully transparent outside the oval. No background, no floor, no cast shadow, no
surrounding room.
```

## Negative prompt

```
cards, playing cards, poker chips, chip trays, dealer button, money, dice, text, letters,
numbers, logos, table markings, inlay, betting lines, insurance line, cup holders, chairs,
players, hands, arms, green felt, green baize, red felt, casino carpet, wood grain, floor,
room, background, cast shadow, vignette, perspective, three-quarter view, angled view,
tilted, glossy, reflective, wet, neon, glow, lens flare, sparkles, watermark
```

## Output spec

- **1024 × 1536 PNG, transparent outside the oval.** The oval fills the frame edge to edge
  with no margin.
- Filename `table.png`, downscaled to `public/art/table.webp` at 700 × 1050 before
  committing — the table renders at most 350 CSS px wide, so that is already 2×.
- The component's container is `aspect-[5/8]`; this asset is 2:3. I adjust the container to
  match rather than stretching the weave, since a stretched texture is the one thing that
  will read as fake at this size.

## How it gets used

The CSS gradient stays underneath as the base and the fallback. The image layers on top with
the existing `.felt-panel` glow still applied over it, so the violet light stays a token
rather than being baked into a file — if the accent ever moves, the table follows without
regenerating art.

## Pot stack and playing cards

The 2026-09-13 memorable-hand picker supersedes the earlier “cards are not rendered” note.
Card faces are still drawn in code: the picker needs all 52 exact rank/suit combinations, and
raster-generated lettering is less reliable and less accessible than real text. Their visual
direction comes from the supplied Moon Poker reference: pale lavender-white stock, oversized
sparse ranks and suit marks, rounded corners, and a soft violet-cast shadow.

Denomination chips also remain code-drawn because a host may choose any body and edge colour.
The pot stack is the exception: it has three fixed colours and is an atmospheric illustration,
so it benefits from the same matte 3D material as the avatar characters.

### Pot stack — `public/art/states/pot-stack.webp`

Generated with the built-in ImageGen tool using the old outlined stack as a composition
reference and `avatar-aner.webp` plus `states/counting.webp` as material/style references.

```
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: small game UI icon for the pot at the center of a mobile poker table
Primary request: replace the minimal outlined three-chip stack with a polished stack of three
physical poker chips that belongs beside the chip-creature characters
Scene/backdrop: genuinely transparent background
Subject: three poker chips stacked at a slight three-quarter angle, one charcoal chip, one
coral-red chip, and one jade-green chip, each with cream edge inserts; readable as a single
compact silhouette at 48–64 CSS pixels
Style/medium: stylized 3D game asset; soft matte clay and painted resin; rounded forms; the same
sculpted material, soft key light, deep ambient fill, and subtle cool-violet rim light as the
character references
Composition/framing: centered, compact, nearly square; stack fills the canvas with a small even
margin; no cast shadow outside the object
Lighting/mood: broad soft key from upper left, gentle cool violet rim from upper right
Color palette: charcoal #26262F, coral #FF4D6A, jade #1FB97A, cream #F5F6F7
Materials/textures: smooth matte molded resin with subtle bevels, no gloss
Constraints: transparent alpha background; exactly three chips; no faces, no arms, no legs, no
denomination, no text, no numbers, no logos, no watermark
Avoid: thin outline icon, flat vector rings, casino branding, money, cards, dice, table, scenery,
extra chips, photorealistic casino photography
```

# 3. App icon and wordmark

## The idea

A clock tower, leaning. It carries both meanings of the app's name at once — the landmark the
name nods to, and what "on tilt" means at a poker table — and the lean is what makes it a mark
rather than a picture of a building.

**The clock face is a poker chip.** Same edge spots as `PokerChip` draws, with the hands
reading across it. That is the single detail tying the tower to the product; without it this is
a clock tower for any app.

Two clock hands, positioned to read as roughly **two o'clock** — the hour the settlement
actually happens, and an angle that leans the same way the tower does instead of fighting it.

*Note: this is an original mark. It is not a reproduction of any existing game's building —
only the general idea of a leaning clock tower, which no one owns.*

## Prompt — app icon

```
Flat 2D vector app icon. A simple clock tower seen straight on, leaning about eight
degrees to the right, built from three stacked rectangular blocks that narrow towards the
top, capped by a plain pitched roof — no ornament, no windows, no brickwork, no texture.

Set into the widest block is a large circular clock face rendered as a poker chip: a solid
disc with six evenly spaced rectangular notches cut into its rim, a plain inner ring, and
two straight hands with no numerals, pointing to roughly two o'clock.

Pure flat colour, hard edges, no gradients, no shading, no highlights, no outlines, no
perspective, no depth. Bold enough to read as a solid silhouette at sixteen pixels.
Centred, with generous even margin on all four sides.

Colours: a single warm off-white #F5F6F7 shape on a solid deep charcoal #0B0B0F square.
The clock face's disc is charcoal, its rim notches and hands off-white.
```

## Negative prompt

```
3D, isometric, perspective, depth, shading, gradient, glossy, bevel, drop shadow, inner
shadow, outline, stroke, texture, brickwork, stonework, roof tiles, windows, arches,
numerals, roman numerals, text, letters, words, watermark, signature, realistic,
photograph, render, sky, clouds, ground, landscape, city, buildings, people, playing
cards, multiple towers, ornate, detailed, filigree, gold, neon, glow
```

## Output spec

- **1024 × 1024 PNG.**
- Two crops from the same artwork:
  - `icon.png` — the tower at about 76% of the frame.
  - `icon-maskable.png` — the tower at about **56%** of the frame, so nothing is lost when
    a launcher clips it to a circle or a squircle. Android will cut roughly 20% off every
    edge, and the current icon does not survive that.
- Also export `logo.svg` if the generator can, or I trace the PNG once — the mark is simple
  enough that a vector is worth having for the masthead, where it scales with type.
- Replaces `icon-premium.png`, which is 1.4 MB for an icon.

## The wordmark

The masthead is currently type only. With this mark it becomes a lockup: the tower to the
start of the word, at cap height, leaning into it.

```
TILTED
POKER
```

Set in Rubik, the app's only family — 800 for `TILTED`, and `POKER` in the same size and
weight at `--text-2`, so the second line recedes the way it already does on the home screen.
Tracking at `-0.03em`. No separate display face: a wordmark that needs a font the rest of the
app never loads is a wordmark that never matches the app.

## Where it lands

`src/app/manifest.ts` (both icon entries), the README logo, and the home masthead in
`src/app/page.tsx`.
